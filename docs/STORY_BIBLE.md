# Beast Taming — Story Bible (Working Canon)

> Purpose: preserve the author's established premise and character decisions so future outlining, drafting and AI-assisted continuity work do not overwrite or accidentally invent canon.
>
> Status key:
> - **Established** — directly chosen or stated by the author.
> - **Working direction** — a concept from the author's route that appears intended to remain, but whose exact mechanics are not yet finalized.
> - **Undecided** — not specified yet. Do not fill these gaps as facts without the author's approval.
> - **Dropped / superseded** — an earlier mechanism the author explicitly moved away from.

## 1. Core premise

**Established:** The world of the story is organized around seven major elemental beast-taming paths/families. The seven major elements are:

1. Dark
2. The other six major elements — their individual names and classifications have not yet been specified.

The protagonist belongs to the **Dark Family**, one of the seven elemental families.

The story begins from the position of a family that has been politically and socially weakened. The Dark Family is treated as dangerous or inferior by the other six, while the apparent moral order of the world is not what it seems.

## 2. The Dark Family's history and central secret

**Established / working direction:**

- The Dark Family was betrayed by the other six elemental families.
- In the past, the head of the Dark Family made a promise. Because of that promise, the family is bound to comply with demands imposed by the other six families.
- This obligation continues even as compliance damages the Dark Family's ability to survive. The family is being pushed toward decline and potentially extinction.
- The Dark Family's true potential is greater than the world believes: its members can cultivate not only Dark beasts, but beasts of **all seven elements**.
- This ability is the reason the other six families fear the Dark Family. The public hostility and suppression are connected to fear of what the Dark Family could become if its true capability returned.

**Important story tension:** The Dark Family is not simply weak. It is constrained, politically cornered and carrying a hidden capability that could overturn the existing balance of power.

**Undecided:**
- The exact wording, conditions, loopholes and consequences of the ancestral promise.
- Why the former head made the promise, whether it was voluntary, coerced or made to prevent something worse.
- The exact historical betrayal and the role each of the six families played.
- How the all-seven-elements cultivation ability works, why it was hidden, and what limits or costs it has.
- Whether every Dark Family member can potentially cultivate all seven elements or whether the potential requires a particular condition.

## 3. Moral theme and genre direction

**Established:** The intended moral contrast is:

- **Dark that appears evil may actually be good.**
- **Light that appears good may actually be corrupted.**

The story should challenge the reader's first impression of factions, symbols, reputations and public morality. The Dark Family should not automatically be portrayed as virtuous in every action, nor should every Light-aligned character be evil. The central intention is to reverse the world's simplistic public judgement: the supposedly righteous side may be protecting a corrupt order, while the feared side may have been made into a villain by history and propaganda.

This theme should influence:
- How the six other families describe the Dark Family.
- How ordinary people understand the seven elements.
- The difference between public history and hidden history.
- The motives behind apparently noble policies and apparently cruel decisions.
- The protagonist's gradual understanding of who benefits from the existing order.

## 4. Protagonist

### Established profile

- **Age:** 16.
- **Family position:** The protagonist is the patriarch's eleventh child.
- **Birth position:** He is the third son of a concubine (the fourth concubine).
- **Maternal branch:** The author described the protagonist as having two other siblings in this branch. The exact sibling order, genders and whether both share the same mother should be confirmed before being treated as fixed details.
- **Social position:** He is bullied and looked down upon because his mother is regarded as an illegitimate or disfavoured concubine.
- **Elemental affiliation:** He belongs to the Dark Family.

### Character starting point

The protagonist begins from a low-status position inside a powerful but declining family. His treatment is shaped not only by the Dark Family's reputation in the wider world, but also by internal household hierarchy and the stigma attached to his mother's position.

His eleventh-child status and his mother's standing create a natural gap between his formal family identity and the respect he receives in daily life.

### Working story direction: the ancient beast soul

An earlier version of the premise included an ancient beast soul selecting the protagonist around his coming-of-age ceremony and becoming his cultivation host. The soul needs its host to grow stronger so that it can eventually manifest in the physical world.

The ancient soul also reveals the Dark Family's founding secret: the family can cultivate beasts of all seven elements, not only Dark beasts.

**Current status:** The ancient beast soul and its role are a working direction from the earlier concept. The exact selection scene, trigger, identity, limitations, relationship with the protagonist and manifestation requirements remain undecided. Do not assume the old artifact-gamble scene is still part of the plot.

## 5. The protagonist's mother

### Established profile

- She was formerly a talented beast tamer.
- Her cultivation was crippled.
- She is the protagonist's mother and is associated with the fourth-concubine branch.
- Her status contributes to the protagonist being bullied and treated as illegitimate or lesser within the household.

### Story importance

Her past creates a contrast between former talent and present powerlessness. She can connect the protagonist's personal family story to the larger themes of lost potential, suppressed ability and the difference between a person's real worth and their current standing.

**Undecided:**
- Who crippled her cultivation and why.
- Whether the injury was political, personal, a consequence of a beast contract or something else.
- Whether she knows the Dark Family's all-seven-elements secret.
- Whether she deliberately hides her former abilities or has been forced into silence.
- Her personality, current goals and relationship with the patriarch.
- Whether her cultivation can be restored, and what restoration would cost.

These are development questions, not established facts.

## 6. The patriarch / father

### Established profile

- He is the current family head/patriarch and the protagonist's father.
- He is cold and ambitious.
- He values strength above family, at least in the way he presents and conducts himself.
- He is secretly overprotective of each of his children.

### Character tension

The two sides of his characterization should coexist rather than cancel each other out:

- **Public and visible side:** cold, demanding, ambitious, strength-focused and difficult to approach.
- **Private and concealed side:** deeply protective of his children, even if he rarely expresses that protection through warmth.

His protection does not automatically make him gentle or fair. He may still make harsh decisions, impose severe expectations or allow household hierarchies to hurt people, while believing that strength is the only reliable way to keep his children alive in a hostile world.

This creates a useful family conflict: his children may interpret his coldness as indifference, while his hidden actions reveal that he is watching over them.

**Undecided:**
- Why he maintains the cold persona.
- Whether his ambition is for personal power, the survival of the Dark Family, protection of his children or several motives at once.
- How he privately protects each child.
- Whether he knows the full truth about the ancestral promise and the all-seven-elements ability.
- His relationship with the protagonist's mother and the other concubines.
- Whether he treats all children equally in protection, opportunity and inheritance.

## 7. Siblings and household conflict

### Established profile

- The protagonist is the patriarch's eleventh child.
- He is the third son of the fourth concubine.
- The author described two other siblings in the same maternal branch; their exact details remain to be fixed.
- At least one sibling secretly resents the protagonist.
- The protagonist is bullied because his mother is considered an illegitimate/disfavoured concubine.

### Relationship direction

The resentment should be a concealed source of tension, not automatically an openly declared rivalry. The sibling's outward behaviour, private motive and eventual choices still need to be developed.

Potential sources of resentment to consider (not canon until selected):
- Feeling overshadowed by the protagonist.
- Believing the mother gives the protagonist special attention.
- Fear that the protagonist may receive an opportunity or inheritance.
- Resentment connected to the mother's fallen status.
- A belief that the protagonist's existence worsens the branch's standing.

Do not select one of these motives without author approval.

### Wider household dynamics

The protagonist's position creates room to explore:
- The hierarchy among the patriarch's children.
- Rivalry and alliances between children of different concubines.
- The social standing of each maternal branch.
- How the patriarch's strength-first values shape competition.
- Whether the children understand their father's secret protectiveness.
- Whether bullying is tolerated, ignored or strategically used as a test.

The exact number of children, identities of the other concubines, sibling names, ages, beast contracts and inheritance rules are not yet established in the available discussion.

## 8. Earlier coming-of-age / artifact idea — status clarification

An earlier plot version had the current patriarch gamble or risk an ancestral artifact during a family gathering around the protagonist's coming-of-age ceremony. An ancient beast soul would emerge and select the protagonist.

**The author later said to forget the head gambling with the artifact** and redirected the focus toward the protagonist's position as the third son of a concubine and eleventh child.

Therefore:
- Do **not** write the patriarch gambling the ancestral artifact as current canon.
- Do **not** build the opening around that gamble.
- The ancient beast soul remains an earlier working concept, but its arrival mechanism must be redesigned or confirmed.
- The coming-of-age ceremony may remain a possible milestone, but its exact role is not fixed by the current decisions.

## 9. Main conflict layers

These are story-development layers implied by the established premise. They are organizing lenses, not additional canon events.

### Personal conflict
A sixteen-year-old boy is bullied and underestimated because of his mother's low household status. He must discover his own value and decide what kind of person he wants to become.

### Family conflict
The protagonist lives inside a household where status, concubine hierarchy, sibling resentment and the patriarch's harsh strength-first philosophy shape everyday life. Hidden protection and visible coldness complicate the relationships.

### Family-survival conflict
The Dark Family is bound by an ancestral promise to obey demands from the other six families. Continuing to obey threatens its survival.

### Historical / political conflict
The public account of the Dark Family and the actual history of betrayal may be different. The other six families have a reason to fear the Dark Family's true ability.

### Elemental / cultivation conflict
The accepted order treats the seven elements as distinct major paths, while the Dark Family's hidden all-seven-elements potential challenges that structure.

### Moral conflict
The protagonist must learn to distinguish genuine virtue from the appearance of righteousness, and genuine evil from the reputation of darkness.

## 13. Protagonist psychological foundation — author-approved direction (2026-11-28)

**Development route:** Route D — Complete Fantasy Saga.  
**Working approach:** Editorial-led collaboration; the author retains final approval.

The author approved the following connected psychological foundation for the protagonist:

1. **Primary wound — conditional worth:** He wants to become worthy in the eyes of others and believes he must become valuable enough that nobody can treat him as disposable.
2. **Defensive behaviour — resistance to control:** He resists being controlled, ordered around or treated as property. This is his protective armour, not the deepest wound.
3. **Eventual moral dilemma — fear of becoming cruel:** As he gains strength, he must confront the possibility that he is reproducing the strength-first cruelty that shaped him.

**Editorial synthesis:** The wound is conditional worth; the defensive armour is resistance to control; the moral consequence is the danger of becoming cruel. These are one connected character arc, not three separate arcs.

**Status boundary:** Only this three-part psychological combination and its connected interpretation are author-approved at this stage. The specific misbelief wording, external and deeper desires, coping mechanisms, personality traits, habits, physical responses, opening goal and relationship interpretations in `docs/PROTAGONIST_DEVELOPMENT_BLUEPRINT.md` are proposals or open questions unless separately approved.

The protagonist must not be reduced to a perfect underdog. He may feel envy, resentment, shame, pettiness and the desire to retaliate. His suffering explains some behaviour but does not excuse harm he causes. His transformation must be gradual and may regress under pressure.

**Thematic bridge (proposal):** His development may connect to beast taming through the question of whether he can build a genuine bond with a creature that has its own will while he still believes strength determines whose wishes matter. This does not establish a first beast, species, contract, ancient soul event or cultivation mechanic.

**Detailed development record:** See [Protagonist Development Blueprint — Phase 1](PROTAGONIST_DEVELOPMENT_BLUEPRINT.md). The Story Bible remains the authority for established facts; the blueprint separates approved decisions, proposals and unresolved questions.
