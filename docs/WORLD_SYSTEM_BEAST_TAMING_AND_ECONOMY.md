# World System 03 — Beast Taming, Contracts and the Beast Economy

**Status: Working system proposal.** This document records the author's established direction and structured proposals. Items marked proposal remain adjustable until explicitly approved.

## 1. Foundational direction from the author

- Beast taming combines control and partnership. Beasts have their own will, but contracts can also provide a means of command or control.
- Each major elemental family maintains particular compatible beast species or family beast lineages.
- Most members cultivate beasts associated with their own family's established lineages rather than personally searching the wilderness for a suitable beast.
- Searching for a suitable wild beast is possible but requires substantial expense, exploration and risk.
- Mercenaries participate in exploration and beast acquisition.
- Beast markets exist and have economic importance.

## 2. The relationship between human and beast

A beast is a living creature with its own instincts, preferences, intelligence, temperament and will. A contract creates a supernatural mana connection, but should not erase the beast's individuality by default.

Distinguish three dimensions:

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Contract** | A supernatural connection or agreement between human and beast. |
| **Control** | The ability to direct, command or restrain a beast. |
| **Bond** | Trust, loyalty, affection and mutual understanding developed between the two. |

These dimensions can differ. A beast may obey without loyalty, trust without complete obedience, or cooperate because the relationship benefits both parties. A strong emotional bond is not automatically proof of a strong contract, and a strong contract does not guarantee affection.

This distinction should shape characterization: some tamers regard beasts as weapons or property; others treat them as partners, companions or family.

## 3. Proposed contract categories

The following are useful conceptual categories. Their exact names, legal status, mechanics and prevalence remain open.

### Voluntary Contract
A beast willingly accepts the connection.
- The beast retains its will and personality.
- Cooperation can develop naturally.
- Trust may grow over time.
- Voluntary acceptance does not guarantee obedience in every situation.

### Subjugation Contract
The tamer establishes dominance and compels obedience.
- The beast may be forced to follow commands.
- Obedience need not imply loyalty.
- Maintaining control may require sufficient cultivation strength, technique or mana.
- A powerful or unwilling beast may resist, exploit weaknesses or challenge control.

### Mutual Partnership Contract
Both parties willingly accept a deeper cooperative relationship.
- Mana synchronization may become more efficient.
- Cooperation can become more intuitive.
- Both parties may benefit from shared cultivation or other contract functions.
- Conflict, betrayal or incompatible goals may strain the relationship.

A partnership should be a high-quality relationship, not necessarily the rarest contract category. A powerful subjugated beast is not automatically more useful or valuable than a weaker beast that cooperates with genuine trust.

## 4. Proposed functions and limits of a contract

A contract may provide some combination of:
1. Recognition of the parties and their connection.
2. A mana link between human and beast.
3. Communication of basic intentions, emotions or commands.
4. Mana synchronization or limited energy exchange.
5. A means of calling, recalling or locating the contracted beast, if the contract technique supports it.

These functions are not all confirmed. Do not assume every contract automatically grants telepathy, storage, summoning, shared senses, shared injuries or shared lifespan. Each function requires a later worldbuilding decision.

The contract's limits, cost, range, consent requirements, termination rules and consequences of death or betrayal remain open.

## 5. Family Beast Lineages

Each of the seven major elemental families maintains particular beast species, sub-species or bloodlines with high compatibility for its members and cultivation methods. These established lineages are called **Family Beast Lineages** in this document as a provisional term.

They are not necessarily one individual beast or one exclusive species. A family may maintain several species and branches of a lineage.

Possible advantages:
- **Elemental compatibility:** established species tend to synchronize well with the family's elemental cultivation methods.
- **Accumulated knowledge:** generations have documented the species' temperament, needs, weaknesses, growth and evolution.
- **Breeding:** the family can maintain breeding grounds and preserve desirable traits.
- **Supply and access:** members can obtain suitable young beasts without funding dangerous independent expeditions.
- **Military organization:** the family can train and deploy familiar species and their tamers.
- **Economic independence:** the family reduces reliance on outside sellers for its most common beast partners.

Family lineages are compatible options, not guarantees of a successful bond. An individual beast may reject a candidate, and an individual family member may be poorly suited to the lineage.

### Illustrative family beast lineages

These are examples only; names and assignments are not canon:
| Family | Element | Possible species |
|---|---|---|
| Dark | Dark | Shadow Panther, Abyssal Wolf |
| Light | Light | Radiant Stag, Sacred Griffin |
| Fire | Fire | Flame Lion, Inferno Hawk |

Do not use these example species in chapters as established facts until the author selects them.

## 6. Family selection and allocation

**Proposal:** Major families operate a selection or allocation process for young members when they reach the appropriate cultivation stage or age.

A family may consider:
- Elemental affinity and compatibility with available lineages.
- Mana Core development and cultivation technique.
- Physical health and capacity to support a beast.
- Temperament and likely partnership suitability.
- The family's available young beasts and breeding resources.
- Household status, political influence and resource allocation rules.

The process need not be perfectly fair. Influential branches may receive first choice, better specimens or more resources. Less influential households may receive fewer options or lower-priority allocations.

A beast should retain some agency in the process. It may accept, reject, resist or show preference toward a candidate, depending on the contract and handling method.

The age, ceremony format, selection rights, number of beasts and allocation rules have not yet been decided.

## 7. Why tamers seek wild beasts

Family lineages offer convenience, known characteristics and established methods, but they do not contain every possible beast or guarantee the best match for every individual.

Reasons to seek wild beasts include:
- Unique or unusual abilities not found in common family lineages.
- Rare or ancient bloodlines.
- Exceptional individual compatibility.
- Unusual mutation or evolution potential.
- Lack of access to suitable family beasts.
- Family restrictions, political exclusion or limited allocation.
- Personal preference or the desire to discover an independent partner.

Wild exploration is expensive and dangerous. A successful expedition may produce a valuable beast, but there is no guarantee that the group will find one, capture it alive or establish a contract with it.

## 8. The cost and risk of wild-beast exploration

A serious expedition may require:
- Experienced guides and scouts.
- Maps, local intelligence and habitat records.
- Food, shelter and survival equipment.
- Mana crystals and other energy supplies.
- Medicine and healing resources.
- Protective equipment and combat support.
- Tracking specialists.
- Capture tools and suitable contract materials.
- Transport, storage or safe containment arrangements.
- Payment for mercenaries, escorts and specialists.
- Compensation for injuries, losses or failed missions.

An expedition can return empty-handed. It may suffer injuries, lose equipment, lose a beast, or result in fatalities. A captured beast may also prove unsuitable, unhealthy, too dangerous or unwilling to accept a contract.

This makes wild-beast acquisition a high-risk, potentially high-reward undertaking.

## 9. Mercenary and exploration industry

Mercenaries are not only combatants. They support the wider beast economy as explorers, trackers, hunters, capturers, escorts and specialists.

Possible professional roles:

- **Beast Hunters:** locate, track and hunt beasts; some specialize in obtaining materials from dead beasts.
- **Beast Scouts:** map dangerous regions, locate habitats, nests, migration routes and rare sightings.
- **Beast Capturers:** specialize in taking beasts alive while minimizing injury.
- **Beast Escorts:** protect merchants, researchers, expeditions and caravans.
- **Beast Merchants:** purchase, evaluate, transport and sell beasts.
- **Beast Breeders:** maintain breeding facilities and cultivate desirable bloodlines.
- **Appraisers:** assess species, rank, health, bloodline, potential and authenticity.
- **Handlers and Healers:** care for captured or injured beasts and prepare them for transfer or rehabilitation.

Mercenary guilds may maintain mission records, reputation grades, expedition contracts, territory intelligence and reward systems. The exact guild structure and ranking system remain open.

## 10. The Beast Market

A substantial beast market connects wild acquisition, family breeding, independent sellers and prospective tamers.

Potential market categories:
- **Eggs:** unhatched beasts, where the species reproduces this way.
- **Young Beasts:** immature creatures that may adapt to a tamer while growing.
- **Mature Beasts:** developed individuals with known abilities and established habits.
- **Rare Bloodlines:** beasts carrying unusual or valuable inherited traits.
- **Mutated Beasts:** individuals with abnormal characteristics or unusual development.
- **Injured Beasts:** lower-priced or specialized rehabilitation cases.
- **Uncontracted Captured Beasts:** living wild creatures legally offered for evaluation and sale.
- **Breeding Stock:** selected individuals used to maintain or improve a family or commercial lineage.

Not every category applies to every species. Do not assume all beasts lay eggs, can be bred in captivity or can be safely transported.

### Beast valuation

Price may depend on:
- Species rarity and demand.
- Current cultivation rank.
- Bloodline quality and pedigree.
- Potential for growth or evolution.
- Elemental compatibility with likely buyers.
- Health, injuries and condition.
- Intelligence, temperament and handling difficulty.
- Existing contract status and legal ownership.
- Rarity of abilities and reliability of appraisal evidence.

Current rank and future potential are different. A low-rank young beast with a rare bloodline or exceptional potential may command a higher price than a high-rank beast with ordinary prospects. Such claims should be supported by reliable appraisal rather than seller promises.

## 11. Family allocation, commercial purchase and wild exploration

Three broad acquisition routes shape the economy:

### Family allocation
- Usually the normal route for members of major families.
- May be subsidized or supported by family breeding facilities.
- Offers established knowledge and cultivation guidance.
- Access and quality may depend on household status, merit, age or family resources.
- Is not necessarily free or fair.

### Commercial purchase
- Available through beast markets and private merchants.
- Offers a wider range of species and individual traits.
- Prices depend on supply, demand, quality and risk.
- Buyers must consider fraud, hidden injuries, false pedigree and inaccurate claims about potential.

### Wild exploration
- Offers the possibility of unusual discoveries and unique partners.
- Requires substantial upfront funding, expertise and risk.
- Has no guarantee of success.
- May be the only route to certain rare wild individuals or undiscovered lineages.

These routes create differences in wealth and opportunity. A privileged heir may receive a carefully selected family beast; an ordinary cultivator may save for years to buy one; an ambitious poor cultivator may join a mercenary expedition to seek a partner independently.

## 12. The lifetime cost of beast cultivation

Acquiring a beast is only the beginning. Long-term ownership and cultivation require continuing resources.

| Expense | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Mana Crystals | Support mana accumulation or cultivation, if suitable for the species |
| Beast Food | Maintain health, growth and species-specific nutrition |
| Medicine | Treat injuries, illness and cultivation complications |
| Evolution Materials | Support particular breakthroughs or transformations |
| Training | Develop abilities, discipline and coordination |
| Habitat and Housing | Provide suitable living conditions |
| Equipment | Armor, harnesses, transport and specialized tools |
| Specialist Services | Veterinary care, appraisal, handling and instruction |

A family or tamer may afford to acquire a rare beast but lack the resources to cultivate it properly. This creates tension between acquisition prestige and long-term responsibility.

Different species may require very different diets, habitats, materials and care. Do not assume all beasts consume mana crystals or share identical needs.

## 13. Death, transfer, ownership and illegal trade

**Proposals for later legal and system design:**
- A beast with an active contract should not be freely sold as ordinary property without resolving the contract.
- Contract termination or transfer should require defined conditions and safeguards.
- A beast that voluntarily leaves a partnership should not automatically become the former tamer's property.
- A dead beast may leave a Mana Core and other valuable materials, subject to the world's harvesting rules.
- Captured beasts should have traceable ownership and legitimate capture rights.
- Illegal trafficking, stolen beasts, forged pedigrees and black-market trade may exist, especially for rare bloodlines.
- A living beast with extraordinary long-term potential may be worth more than its harvested remains, depending on demand and condition.

The legal status of beasts, their rights, transfer consent, forced capture and contract enforcement must be developed later. Do not assume that every society treats beasts ethically or consistently.

## 14. The wider economic cycle

The beast economy can operate as a connected system:

1. Wild habitats and family breeding grounds sustain beast populations.
2. Scouts, hunters and capturers locate or acquire specimens.
3. Breeders preserve and develop selected lineages.
4. Mercenary groups transport, escort or recover beasts.
5. Merchants and markets evaluate and distribute them.
6. Families and independent tamers acquire beasts through allocation, purchase or personal exploration.
7. Tamers invest in food, mana resources, medicine, training and evolution materials.
8. Beasts support cultivation, missions, transport, defense, warfare and other services.
9. Trade, rewards and harvested materials return wealth to the wider economy.

This creates multiple professions and economic dependencies rather than making beast taming an isolated personal hobby.

## 15. Story opportunities involving the protagonist

Possible uses of this system in the established family story:
- Influential legitimate branches receive first choice of the family's best young beasts.
- A less influential concubine household may receive a restricted allocation or fewer resources.
- The protagonist's mother may lack the wealth or political influence to secure a premium family beast for him.
- The protagonist may be offered a beast others dismiss as ordinary, defective or unsuitable.
- A wild beast may recognize an unusual quality in him and reject other candidates.
- His unusual compatibility may challenge the family's established breeding and selection assumptions.
- A beast that appears weak may possess hidden potential, creating a parallel to the protagonist's own social position.

These are plot possibilities, not events that have already happened.

A further thematic possibility is that the Dark Family's ancestral method emphasized voluntary partnership and mutual development, while rival families increasingly favored predictable control and military obedience. This could make the Dark method appear inefficient or weak to outsiders while preserving an older philosophy of cooperation. It remains a mystery candidate, not confirmed history.

## 16. Continuity rules for future chapter writers

1. Beasts possess their own will; a contract does not automatically erase individuality.
2. Keep contract, control and emotional bond conceptually distinct.
3. Do not assume every contract provides every possible function such as summoning, telepathy or shared senses.
4. Most members of the major families generally cultivate established compatible Family Beast Lineages rather than searching independently in the wild.
5. Family compatibility does not guarantee that an individual beast will accept an individual tamer.
6. Wild-beast exploration is expensive, dangerous and uncertain.
7. Mercenaries, scouts, hunters, capturers, merchants and breeders are part of the beast economy.
8. Beast markets trade different kinds of living beasts and related stock; species do not all reproduce or develop identically.
9. Current rank and future potential are separate valuation factors.
10. Beast cultivation has continuing costs after acquisition.
11. Do not treat a contracted beast as freely transferable property without established contract and legal rules.
12. Do not assign the protagonist a specific beast, contract type or family allocation outcome until the author confirms it.
13. New contract mechanics, legal rules or market standards introduced in a chapter must be flagged as proposed canon changes for author approval.

## 17. Open design questions

- What is the exact process for forming a beast contract?
- Does the beast have to consent for any contract, or can subjugation bypass consent?
- What are the risks and costs of forced subjugation?
- Can a tamer maintain multiple beast contracts, and what limits the number?
- Can a beast reject, break or renegotiate a contract?
- What happens if the tamer or beast dies?
- Can contracts be transferred, inherited or temporarily suspended?
- How does mana synchronization benefit each party, and what are its limits?
- What is the age or cultivation requirement for the family beast selection?
- Are Family Beast Lineages exclusive to their families, or can outsiders acquire them?
- How are family breeding grounds managed and protected?
- How are beast eggs, young beasts and mature beasts transported and housed?
- What are the legal rights of beasts in different societies?
- How are wild capture permits, territory claims and market ownership verified?
- What are the guild ranks, mission grades and mercenary reward systems?
- How are beast prices standardized and appraised?
- What are the risks of beast trafficking, counterfeit pedigrees and illegal contract removal?
- What exactly is the Dark Family's ancestral philosophy and technique for beast partnership?

The contract categories, contract functions, family selection process, species examples, market classifications, professional roles, pricing factors, legal concepts and possible Dark Family philosophy are structured proposals. They must remain adjustable until explicitly confirmed by the author.
